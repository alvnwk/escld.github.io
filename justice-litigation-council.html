Justice and Litigation Council

Studying disputes. Pursuing justice. Upholding the rule of law.

The Justice and Litigation Council (JLC) is a specialised academic body dedicated to the study of litigation, judicial remedies, procedural strategy, and the protection of rights and freedoms. It seeks to deepen legal understanding of dispute resolution, access to justice, and the role of judicial institutions in upholding the rule of law.

Areas of work

Litigation across jurisdictions.

The Council may organise its work around several complementary fields, depending on members’ interests and the projects approved.

  • Administrative litigation: judicial review of administrative action, interim relief, and public freedoms.
  • Constitutional litigation: constitutional review, constitutional complaints where available, and questions of constitutional procedure.
  • European litigation: procedural frameworks and remedies within the European Union and the Council of Europe.
  • International litigation: the jurisdiction, admissibility requirements, and procedures of international courts and tribunals.
  • Human-rights litigation: the judicial protection of rights and freedoms and the implementation of judgments.
  • Strategic litigation: the study of cases intended to clarify legal principles or advance the protection of rights, where admissible and appropriate.
Organisation

A clear structure, collaborative work.

In line with the ESCLD’s decentralised model, the JLC combines coordination with member-led research. Its structure may evolve as the Council develops.

Coordinates the Council’s programme, convenes meetings, oversees ongoing work, and reports on activities in accordance with the ESCLD’s internal rules.

Working method

How the Council works.

Projects are intended to follow a transparent, research-led process, from defining a legal issue to presenting conclusions.

01

Identify the legal question

Define the issue, relevant facts, applicable legal framework, and scope of the research.

02

Research the law

Review legislation, case law, procedural rules, legal scholarship, and relevant comparative or international materials.

03

Assess options and limitations

Consider jurisdiction, standing, admissibility, deadlines, available remedies, and procedural or practical constraints.

04

Present reasoned conclusions

Produce a memorandum, report, presentation, or publication for discussion and review within the association.

Institutional framework

Research and preparation, within a clear mandate.

The JLC is an academic and advisory body. It may analyse potential disputes, assess possible remedies, and prepare recommendations or draft documents. It does not independently bind the ESCLD, initiate proceedings in the association’s name, or represent the association before a court unless expressly authorised in accordance with the statutes and applicable law.

Any decision to commence or pursue legal proceedings on behalf of the ESCLD must be taken by the competent association body or duly authorised representative. Every potential action remains subject to the relevant rules on jurisdiction, standing, admissibility, and procedure.

Get involved

Bring legal questions into the discussion.

The Council welcomes interest from students and young people keen to explore litigation, procedural law, constitutional review, fundamental rights, or international judicial institutions.

Members may express an interest in contributing to research, suggesting a topic, preparing a case note, or taking part in a future project.
